In the field you rarely have masts or perfect wire. A resonant wire up in the air, roughly the right length, beats any compromise antenna. Here's how to improvise one.

# Field kit
A small bag that turns any trees into a working station:
- Two lengths of wire pre-cut for your main band (with the extra folded back and marked), or one EFHW wire.
- A dipole centre or balun, or an EFHW transformer.
- Two end insulators (or pieces of plastic or dry wood).
- 100 ft (30 m) of paracord and 100 ft of light throw line, with a **throw weight** (a commercial throw bag, or a small cloth bag of sand).
- Coax with connectors, a few feet of extra wire, electrical tape, a small knife.
- A printout of your lengths in case the tablet is busy.

# Getting a line into a tree
[[diagram:throw-line|Throw the weight and light line over a branch, then use it to pull up the paracord, then the antenna.]]

1. Pick a branch that's high, strong and clear of power lines (look up first).
2. Lay the throw line out on the ground in loose loops so it can pay out without tangling.
3. Swing the weight underhand and let go towards the branch. Stand clear; it comes back down.
4. Once it's over, tie the paracord to the throw line and pull it over.
5. Tie the antenna's end insulator to the paracord and haul it up. Tie off to the trunk or a stake with a knot you can undo.

> TIP: Leave a loop of slack in the paracord at the bottom, or tie it to a springy branch, so the antenna doesn't snap when the tree moves in wind.

# Quick antennas
- **Inverted V from one tree:** centre up the tree, legs tied down to stakes or low branches. The fastest dependable NVIS antenna.
- **Sloping dipole:** one end high in a tree, the other low; works well and needs only one high point.
- **EFHW into a tree:** transformer at the base, wire up into the branches. Several bands from one wire.
- **Low dipole between bushes or vehicles:** even 6–10 ft (2–3 m) up, a full-size dipole on 40 m or 80 m works as an NVIS antenna for nearby stations. Expect some loss to the ground, but it works.
- **Whip tied back:** on a vehicle or manpack whip, bend the whip back and tie it so it runs roughly horizontal. It sends more signal upward for nearby stations (NVIS) than the whip standing straight up.

# Finding the right length without a meter
If you have no SWR meter or analyser:
- Cut to the calculated length (that's usually close enough to work).
- Listen: a resonant antenna on the right band brings up the background noise and signals noticeably compared with a wrong length.
- If your radio has a built-in tuner, it will usually tune a wire that's roughly right.

# Low-visibility tips
- Dark, thin insulated wire and dark paracord are hard to see against trees.
- A low NVIS antenna is much less visible than a tall one, and works better for nearby stations anyway.
- Lay the coax along the ground, not across paths.
