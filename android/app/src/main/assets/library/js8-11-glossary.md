# A to F
- **ACK:** an automatic acknowledgement that a whole message arrived.
- **@ALLCALL:** the group that means everyone listening.
- **Antenna:** the wire or rod that sends and receives radio waves. See the Antennas guides.
- **Balun / unun:** a small transformer or choke where coax meets the antenna.
- **Band:** a range of frequencies, named by wavelength (40 m, 20 m…).
- **Callsign:** a station's unique identifier, issued with a licence (W1AW, KX4ABC).
- **Choke:** coax coiled or passed through ferrite to stop RF on the outside of the cable.
- **Coax (coaxial cable):** the cable between radio and antenna.
- **CQ:** a general call to anyone.
- **dB (decibel):** a way of comparing strengths. +3 dB ≈ double power; +10 dB = 10×.
- **Dial frequency:** the frequency on the radio's display, e.g. 7.078 MHz.
- **Digest:** this app's summary page of needs, emergencies, unanswered messages and stations.
- **Directed message:** a message addressed to one station or group.
- **Dipole:** a wire antenna fed in the middle; the dependable standard.
- **Duty cycle:** how much of the time the transmitter is on. JS8 is high (about 84%).
- **EFHW:** end-fed half-wave antenna; one support, several bands.
- **Frame:** one piece of a JS8 message, sent in one time slot.
- **Frequency:** how many times per second a radio wave repeats (Hz, kHz, MHz).

# G to P
- **Grid square:** a Maidenhead location code (EM83, EM83KX).
- **Group:** a name starting with @ that a set of stations listens for.
- **HB / Heartbeat:** an automatic "I'm on the air" announcement with your grid.
- **HF:** high frequency, 3 to 30 MHz; signals bend back from the ionosphere and travel far.
- **Ionosphere:** the charged upper atmosphere that bends HF signals back to earth.
- **LSB / USB:** lower / upper sideband. JS8 always uses **USB**.
- **MGRS:** the military grid reference system; a precise location format.
- **MUF:** maximum usable frequency; above it, signals pass through the ionosphere and are lost.
- **NCS:** net control station.
- **Net:** a scheduled on-air gathering of stations, run by an NCS.
- **NVIS:** near vertical incidence skywave; a low antenna that covers 0 to about 400 km.
- **Offset:** where a signal sits in the audio, in Hz (e.g. 1,500 Hz).
- **Propagation:** how radio signals travel, and which paths are open when.
- **PTT:** push-to-talk; switching the radio to transmit.

# Q to Z
- **QRP:** low power, 5 W or less.
- **QSO:** a contact or conversation between stations.
- **Relay:** passing a message through a station that can hear both ends (A>B>MESSAGE).
- **Rig / transceiver:** a radio that can both receive and transmit.
- **Rig control (CAT):** the cable link that lets the app set the radio's frequency and transmit.
- **Skip zone:** the area too far for the direct signal and too close for the sky wave.
- **SNR:** signal-to-noise ratio, a signal report in dB (−24 is very weak, 0 is good).
- **SSB:** single sideband; the efficient way to send voice and data on HF.
- **Store and forward:** leaving a message with a station to be collected later (MSG, MSG TO:, QUERY MSGS).
- **SWR:** standing wave ratio; how well the antenna matches. 1.0 is perfect, under 2 is fine.
- **Time slot / period:** the fixed 6, 10, 15 or 30 seconds each frame occupies.
- **Tuner:** a device that matches an antenna to the radio.
- **UTC / Zulu (Z):** universal time; JS8 traffic uses it for all times.
- **VHF / UHF:** higher frequencies (above 30 MHz) that travel in straight lines; handhelds use them.
- **VOX:** voice-operated transmit; the radio transmits when it hears audio from the app.
- **Waterfall:** the scrolling picture of the audio: offset across, time down.
- **Wavelength (λ):** the distance a wave travels in one cycle; about 300 ÷ MHz metres.
