Antennas hurt people in a few well-known ways. All of them are avoidable.

# Power lines: the one that kills
> WARNING: Never put up an antenna, a mast, or a throw line anywhere it could touch a power line, **even if it falls**. Contact with an overhead line can kill you instantly, through the wire, the rope, the mast or the coax. Assume every overhead line is live, including ones that look like phone or cable lines.

- Keep everything at least the full height of your antenna or mast, plus a good margin, away from any overhead line.
- Look up before you throw a line or raise a mast. Check again at night or in rain, when lines are hard to see.
- If an antenna touches a power line, stay away from it and everything connected to it, and call the power company.

# Lightning and static
- A wire in the air collects static charge, especially before storms. Discharge it (touch the coax centre to the shield) before handling the connector.
- **When a storm is coming, disconnect the coax from the radio** and put the connector outside or on a ground rod. Don't operate during thunderstorms.
- For a permanent station, ground the coax shield to a ground rod where it enters the building, and bond that to the building's electrical ground. Follow local electrical codes.

# High voltage at the wire ends
The ends of a dipole and the far end of an end-fed wire carry hundreds to thousands of volts while you transmit. A touch can cause a painful **RF burn**.
- Keep wire ends out of reach: at least 8 ft (2.5 m) up, or fenced off.
- Don't transmit while anyone is working on the antenna.

# RF exposure
Radio energy heats body tissue. At typical JS8 power (5–50 W) on HF, a wire antenna a few metres from people is well within safe limits. Higher power and antennas close to people need more care.
- Keep antennas away from where people sit or sleep, and away from children and animals.
- In the US, the FCC requires operators to evaluate RF exposure from their stations; the ARRL publishes simple tables and calculators for this.

# Climbing, ropes and masts
- Don't climb trees or towers alone. Use a throw line from the ground whenever you can.
- Stand clear when throwing weights into trees; they come back down.
- Guy masts in three directions and mark guy ropes so people don't walk into them.
